# Connect story decisions to production evidence

Read before you create or revise a storyboard, select references, or diagnose a failed sequence. This is the organizing model. The other references supply detail for each link.

## Carry one beat through the whole chain

The chain starts from the producer brief: theme, audience experience, tone and practical limits. Each beat serves that direction. Technical feasibility and attractive assets do not settle creative intent.

A beat is a meaningful change in the situation or in the viewer's understanding. It can span several shots, and one shot can carry several beats. Give each essential beat a compact record inside the existing treatment or storyboard document:

| Connection | Record the decision and why the next link needs it |
|---|---|
| Dramatic change | What changes, whose choice or which event causes it, and why it matters now. Separate the required story information from the behavior that makes the beat worth filming. This decides what must be staged. |
| Physical prerequisites | Positions, reachable actions, occupied hands, obstacles and world-time changes that permit the event. Cite the staging map. This decides viable viewpoints. |
| Audience information | What the viewer and the character know before and after, and which picture or sound supplies the difference. Name the intended feeling as well. This decides coverage and timing. |
| Coverage and joins | Which shots express the event, why each cut happens, and the action phase, gaze, attention and world state across it. This decides reference views and handles. |
| Execution dependencies | The exact actor, setting, pose, plate and audio versions and the supported method. Separate constraints you intend from constraints actually sent to the backend. |
| Acceptance evidence | The artifact and interval to inspect, the observable relationship that passes, what has actually been seen, and what is still uncertain. |

Link each beat to its shot IDs and asset dependencies. Do not duplicate the manifest. Before rendering, state the uncertainty the work will resolve. After rendering, replace assumptions with observations.

Name the audience promise: what experience the film offers, and which missing relationship would defeat it. Test that relationship's uncertain prerequisite before you polish its dependents. A fallback must keep the beat's function. Removing every exterior shot can also remove the evidence that a journey happened.

## Worked pattern: a missed opportunity

A character means to act at a particular moment and misses it. To make the cause legible:

1. Establish the goal and the available action before the opportunity passes.
2. Stage the real positions: where the character is, what each hand holds, what they could reach, and where the world is in time.
3. Choose deliberately between letting the audience notice the opportunity first (anticipation) and sharing the character's late discovery (surprise). They need different timing.
4. Make every insert and return agree with the map. An insert shows the same object from a compatible camera. A return to the wide keeps grip and action phase while the world moves on. A late attempt uses an available hand and a reachable target.
5. Motivate the reaction with an observable discovery.

Identical backgrounds across distant moments contradict elapsed time, even when the actor matches perfectly.

## State what each cut claims

For adjacent shots, identify whether the cut continues an action, changes viewpoint within the same place, or moves to a different place or time. A separate singer performance can be another clearly established setting. Put this decision beside the existing shot cards, with the landmarks, actor position and facing direction, destination, camera ending/starting view and action phase that must agree.

- A continued route keeps its physical destination. A shallow stair ending at a shutter cannot become a steep stair open to the sky; a closed bay cannot become an open arcade without another route being shown.
- A reaction belongs to the place and event that cause it. Locate the actor and their line of sight to the target. A familiar face beside unrelated shutters does not establish a reaction to a gate elsewhere. A destination behind a front-facing actor does not establish that they are looking at it.
- A changed place needs a readable transition or establishing view. Calling the sequence a montage, or assigning different map letters, does not make a contradictory continuation legible. A performance cutaway can separate spaces; it does not repair wrong geometry inside either space.
- Repeated portraits need distinct visible behavior and useful new information. Matching identity and changing the background alone do not supply a discovery, decision or progression.

Use a selected scene plate for the same camera, and an actual selected out-frame for a continuation. Once motion exists, replace planned ending/starting assumptions with the real retained frames and [review the moving joins](motion-control.md#review-the-moving-joins).

## Diagnose at the earliest broken link

| Observed failure | Trace the cause and repair it upstream |
|---|---|
| The viewer cannot tell why the character reacts | Check that goal, lost opportunity and discovery were shown. Repair story information or coverage before asking for a stronger expression. |
| A contact looks impossible | Check map geometry, hand ownership and approach. The fix can change the wide, insert, references and adjacent action. A tighter crop hides the problem without fixing it. |
| The world looks frozen after an insert | Compare world time, exterior references and actual generated displacement. Matching the actor's pose alone preserves the wrong world. |
| A reveal feels arbitrary | Trace it to earlier audience information and geography. Add an earned plant or change the ending. |
| A cut feels abrupt despite similar poses | Watch attention, intention, action phase, direction, speed and sound across the join. Decide whether coverage, performance or the edit is at fault before regenerating. |
| Recognition or cut timing moves | Reassess linked sound cues, device emphasis, dialogue, ambience perspective and music entry. A picture fix can invalidate sound. |
| An actor drifts between shots | Compare at useful face scale against the actual references and conditioning. A new card cannot fix selected takes. |

A defect can have several causes. Record what is visible separately from the suspected cause, and run the smallest test that tells them apart. Keep working assets, but do not keep a broken premise to protect sunk generation cost.

## Readiness holds per stage

A plan has intentions. A storyboard has still staging. A motion proof covers its tested action and joins. An assembled edit covers timing, performance and meaning. An asset-level pass does not carry over to a beat or a sequence. An upstream change reopens the affected downstream work and approval.
