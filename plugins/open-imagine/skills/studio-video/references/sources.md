# Sources behind the method

Provenance only. You do not need this file to run a production. The instructions in the other references are a synthesis for small generative productions. They are not quotations, industry mandates, or proof that a backend can perform them. Production confidence comes from observed takes and the assembled edit.

The wider study also used a supplied production handbook PDF (pages 5-8, 10-17, 14, 24-26, 29, 35), with authorship and date unverified, plus 21 external sources. It lives in a research report outside this repository. From the PDF, the method takes dependency ownership, visual evidence of progress, feasible alternatives and matching the audience promise. It does not take channel-specific retention timers, abrupt endings, mandatory twists or workplace rhetoric.

## Story, performance and blocking

- [Stéphanie Joalland, Raindance: writing short films](https://raindance.org/7-rules-for-writing-short-films/): economical visual development, goals, dilemmas and choices, written with production cost in mind. It does not require three acts or twists.
- [Alfred Hitchcock: My Own Methods, BFI](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/alfred-hitchcock-my-own-methods): opposing forces, meaningful detail and repetition, and the listener's image as meaning. His proposed *Blackmail* ending is not the released ending.
- [Céline Sciamma, BAFTA lecture](https://www.bafta.org/media-centre/press-releases/screenwriters-lecture-series-2019-celine-sciamma/): make plot-required scenes into scenes worth filming.
- [FSU: Toolkit for Directing Actors](https://fsufilmhandbook.com/toolkit-for-directing-actors/): objectives, circumstances and physical tasks instead of mood commands.
- [Greta Gerwig, DGA](https://www.dga.org/craft/dgaq/issues/1802-spring-2018/gen-next-greta-gerwig): rehearsal as investigation. It is not a reason for unlimited retries.
- [BFI NETWORK / Film Hub Midlands](https://filmhubmidlands.org/make/bfi-network-short-film-fund-faqs-for-filmmakers/): complete story, world, tone and intent. A moral is optional.
- [Jay Holben, ASC: Where Do You Put the Camera?](https://theasc.com/article/shot-craft-where-do-you-put-the-camera/) and [Steven Bernstein, Sony Cine: Scene Blocking](https://sony-cinematography.com/articles/the-cinematographer-and-scene-blocking/): camera follows intent and blocking.
- [Lee Ha Jun and Bong Joon Ho on *Parasite*, Architectural Digest](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/bong-joon-ho-parasite-movie-set-design-interview): sets designed for sightlines as well as meaning.

## Editing, attention and sound

- [Thelma Schoonmaker](https://www.provideocoalition.com/art-of-the-cut-thelma-schoonmaker/): frontal compositions and unequal seated heights in *Silence*.
- [Walter Murch](https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-murch-books/): real-time judgment of out-points, emotion, story and rhythm.
- [Eddie Hamilton on *Top Gun: Maverick*](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/top-gun-maverick-2022/): selection and tonal shifts tied to the character's experience.
- [Sidney Lumet, DGA](https://www.dga.org/craft/dgaq/issues/0305-may-2003/under-the-influence-sidney-lumet): *Network*'s visual style develops with the drama.
- [Adobe: Continuity editing](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/hub/ideas/what-is-continuity-editing-in-film.html): match on action, eye trace, purposeful discontinuity.
- [BFI Education: Reading a short film or scene](https://ugc.futurelearn.com/uploads/files/d3/95/d395220f-bbb0-4e76-810f-e3caca25727b/Reading_a_short_film_or_scene.pdf): framing, movement, light, sound and time/space as review questions.
- [Randy Thom: Designing a Movie for Sound](https://filmsound.org/articles/designing_for_sound.htm): sound takes part in construction instead of rescuing weak scenes late.
- [Mark Mangini Q&A](https://blog.prosoundeffects.com/ask-me-anything-with-award-winning-sound-artist-mark-mangini): early sound participation and complete mix sketches.
- [ScreenSkills: ADR editor](https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/post-production/sound-studios/adr-editor-post-production/): lip sync, performance and acoustic continuity.
- [YouTube Help: audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en): a retention spike can mean interest or confusion.

## Continuity, look and graphics

- ScreenSkills [script supervisor skills](https://www.screenskills.com/skills-checklists/scripted-film-and-tv/script-supervisor-department/script-supervisor-skills/) and [job profile](https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/film-and-tv-drama/technical/script-supervisor-film-and-tv-drama/), [LASSN](https://lassn.org/): breakdowns and continuity records. The state ledger adapts this role.
- ScreenSkills [standby props](https://www.screenskills.com/skills-checklists/scripted-film-and-tv/props-department/standby-props-skills/), [hair and makeup](https://www.screenskills.com/skills-checklists/scripted-film-and-tv/hair-make-up-and-prosthetics-department/hair-and-make-up-artist-skills/) and [costume standby](https://www.screenskills.com/skills-checklists/scripted-film-and-tv/costume-department/standby-costume-assistant-skills/): marks, photographs and state changes by story time.
- [Claire Mathon on *Portrait of a Lady on Fire*](https://www.fdtimes.com/2020/05/17/claire-mathon-afc-portrait-of-a-lady-on-fire/) and [Arianne Phillips interview](https://gagosian.com/quarterly/2023/02/13/interview-fashion-and-art-arianne-phillips/): testing cast, costume and light together.
- Disney Animation [visual development](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/visual-development/) and [hand-drawn animation](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/hand-drawn-animation/): model sheets. Using an actor sheet for generated live action is an adaptation.
- [LTX: character consistency](https://ltx.io/blog/how-to-maintain-character-consistency-in-ai-video): reference conditioning and multi-angle libraries. It does not show that a local API exposes LoRA routes.
- [Krista Lomax](https://www.pushing-pixels.org/2018/05/18/the-art-and-craft-of-screen-graphics-interview-with-krista-lomax.html), [Karen Sori](https://www.pushing-pixels.org/2017/09/20/graphic-design-of-the-circle-interview-with-karen-sori.html) and [Frame.io on text messages](https://blog.frame.io/2024/01/22/texting-messaging-in-movies-and-tv-shows/): story-specific, readable screen graphics. The information-gain test comes from producer feedback, not from these sources.

## Travel and environment

- [Haris Zambarloukos on *Murder on the Orient Express*, Kodak](https://www.kodak.com/en/motion/blog-post/haris-zambarloukos-orient-express/): journey plates with coordinated gimbals.
- ASC [On the Walls](https://theasc.com/article/on-the-walls/) and [How It Ends](https://theasc.com/article/long-road-home-how-it-ends/): playback positions across coverage, and environmental light tied to speed.
- [DNEG on *Bullet Train*, AWN](https://www.awn.com/vfxworld/fast-and-curious-dneg-shoots-works-bullet-train-vfx): mix plates and rendered environments by shot need.

## Previs, layout and turnover

- [The Third Floor previs editors](https://www.provideocoalition.com/art-of-the-cut-with-the-previs-editors-of-the-third-floor/): iterate assets, camera and edit together. Simpler previs suits small projects.
- ScreenSkills [previs artist](https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/visual-effects-vfx/pre-production/previsualisation-previs-artist/), [layout artist](https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/animation/production/layout-artist-animation/) and [first assistant director](https://www.screenskills.com/media/7604/final-first-assistant-director-040423.pdf): 3D animatics, layout from storyboard, realistic schedules and contingencies.
- [Park Road VFX turnover guide](https://workflow.deganz.co.nz/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/PRPP_Guide_to_VFXHandovers_v0.4_20200222.pdf): stable shot/version identities, ranges and handles at handoff.

## Constraint-driven production (added 2026-10-23)

Most of these are second-hand: articles quoting practitioners. The method rests on independent traditions agreeing, not on any single source. Notes with the full evidence labels and rejected claims live in the research report outside this repository.

- Visible speech kept small. [Nick Park on Early Man, AWN](https://www.awn.com/print/animationworld/nick-park-stays-true-his-early-style-early-man) (summary); a 1970s TV-anime staff account of three-drawing mouths and one mover per cut, [Gundam Unofficial](https://gundamunofficial.com/production/gundam_mono.html) (translated, second-hand); Paul Trillo, first-hand, [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/ai-music-video); Tom Cross on sliding picture for sync, [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/art-cut-tom-cross-ace-whiplash-joy-la-la-land/) (summary).
- Voice first, phrase table, animatic against the final track. Glen Keane, [A System for Planning and Timing Animation](https://eclass.aegean.gr/modules/document/file.php/511154/Theory/Glen_Keane_Animation.pdf) (first-hand); Josh Beveridge on final reads before animation, [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/how-spider-man-into-the-spider-verses-technical-wizard-1831179866); Paul Machliss on Baby Driver, [Motion Picture Association](https://www.motionpictures.org/2018/01/baby-drivers-oscar-nominated-editor-paul-machliss-marrying-music-mayhem/) (first-hand) and [Frame.io](https://blog.frame.io/2017/07/31/baby-driver-workflow/).
- One layout per shot from one plan. Ghibli layout exhibition, [Mynavi News](https://news.mynavi.jp/article/20080731-ghibli/) (second-hand: positions, motion and camera per sheet; no source found for a per-layer background speed); Mamoru Oshii on the declared lens, [Ghost in the Shell site interview](https://theghostintheshell.jp/feature/interview02_2) (first-hand); layout schedules on Patlabor 2, [Animation Obsessive](https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/life-under-martial-law); Roger Deakins, [Shot design in prep](https://www.rogerdeakins.com/forums/topic/shot-design-in-prep/) (first-hand); Paperman's rough 3D underlay, [fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=39516) and [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/interviews/exclusive-paperman-interview-director-john-kahrs-63782.html).
- Motion budget and cutting inside the action. Limited animation as a concept, [Animation Obsessive on Tezuka](https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/when-osamu-tezuka-redefined-anime); Spider-Verse holds (Beveridge, above); Nathan Caselton on vertical camera moves, [Definition](https://definitionmagazine.com/features/the-rise-of-vertical-dramas/).
- Low yield. Paul Trillo (above); Shy Kids on Air Head, [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/sora-ai-filmmaker-interview) (first-hand); Shane Carruth on single takes in Primer (first-hand article, via the research notes).
- Scope from inventory and ceiling. Robert Rodriguez on writing from what he had, [Lex Fridman Podcast #465](https://lexfridman.com/robert-rodriguez-transcript/) (first-hand); Charles Ramírez Berg's National Film Registry essay on El Mariachi, [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-film-preservation-board/documents/El-Mariachi_Berg.pdf) (second-hand); Christine Vachon, [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/column-post/making-indie-movies-no-budget/) (first-hand); Jason Blum's budget ceiling, [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/entertainment/in-the-foxlight-paranormal-activity-and-insidious-producer-jason-blum-makes-movie-magic-on-a-tiny-budget) (first-hand); Kelly Reichardt, [Sundance Q&A](https://www.sundance.org/blogs/qa-director-kelly-reichardt-on-making-meeks-cutoff-3/) (first-hand).

- Tall frames. Li Yafei, [Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/2670080.html) (first-hand quotes); S. Riley Moneymaker, [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/vertical-filmmaking-interview) (first-hand); Chazelle's *The Stunt Double* as analysed by [PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/framing-shots-for-vertical-cinema) (analysis of the film, not the makers' words); Snap's *Two Sides*, [Shorty Awards](https://shortyawards.com/12th/two-sides).

- Sync tolerance and sound first. No measurement exists for generated faces.
